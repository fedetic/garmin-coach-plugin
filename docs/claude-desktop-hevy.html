garmin-coach · setup guide

Your own coach in Claude Desktop, with your workouts in Hevy

No Garmin needed. You talk to the coach in the Claude Desktop app. It builds your plan, adjusts it as you train, and writes each week's workouts into your Hevy app as routines you start with one tap. You log lifting in Hevy as you always do, and the coach reads it back to see how the week went.

What you get

A personal coach, not a workout generator.

A plan built around you

Goals turned into numbers (a 100 kg bench, a first pull-up, 8 kg lighter), a roadmap of training blocks, and sessions that fit your days, time and equipment, down to the plates in your garage.

A plan that adapts

Every week it compares what you lifted with what was planned, and changes next week only when the evidence says so: progress, hold, deload, or work around a sore shoulder.

A morning check

A one-minute "should I train today?": how you slept and feel, any pain, then GO, MODIFY (lighter or shorter) or REST, with the specific change.

Specialist review

Every block is reviewed before it starts (programming, recovery, injury risk, nutrition), with calorie and protein targets per block.

Workouts in Hevy

Weights worked out and rounded to what you can load. Nothing to copy by hand.

It is a coach built from sound training principles, not a doctor. Tell it about pain and injuries, and see a professional for anything that worries you.

Set up (about 15 minutes)

1

Prepare your computer (one command)

Open a terminal (Mac: Cmd + Space, type Terminal. Windows: Windows key, type PowerShell, not "as administrator"), paste the line for your computer and press Enter. It checks for what the coach needs and installs what's missing: git, and uv, a small tool that runs the helper. On a Mac, git comes with Apple's developer tools: a pop-up appears, finish it and run the command again.

Mac
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fedetic/garmin-coach-plugin/main/scripts/setup.sh | bash -s -- --desktop
Windows (PowerShell)
$env:COACH_TOOL="desktop"; irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fedetic/garmin-coach-plugin/main/scripts/setup.ps1 | iex

It then asks two questions:

  1. Do you have a Garmin watch? Answer n.
  2. Do you log your workouts in the Hevy app? Answer y, then paste your Hevy key when asked. Nothing shows as you type.
Your Hevy keyIn Hevy's web app (hevy.com) open Settings, then Developer, and create an API key (hevy.com/settings?developer). The setup command asks for it once and saves it only on your computer, in ~/.hevy/api-key, readable only by you. Never paste the key into the chat: the coach never needs you to.

The command is safe to run again if it stops halfway.

2

Add the coach to Claude Desktop

In Claude Desktop, open Cowork, go to Plugins (under Customize), choose Add marketplace, and enter:

Marketplace
fedetic/garmin-coach-plugin

Then install garmin-coach. Screen names change between versions; Anthropic's guide is Install plugins.

The plugin also lists a garmin connector. You don't use it. If Desktop shows it as failed or asks you to connect, ignore it or switch it off.

3

Open your coaching folder

Start a new Cowork task and, when it asks for a folder, choose coach in your home folder (the setup command created it). Your goals, plan and history live there as plain files, so you can read them yourself and nothing is locked inside the plugin.

4

Say hello

Type this in the task
set me up as a new athlete

Your first conversation

About 20 to 30 minutes, once. The coach asks one topic at a time:

  1. Your apps. "Do you have a Garmin watch?" (no) and "Do you log in Hevy?" (yes). If you wear another watch, say which.
  2. What it can see. It reads your last 12 weeks from Hevy and tells you what it found: how often you train, your main lifts and heaviest sets, whether you log effort. Correct anything it got wrong.
  3. You. Goals and deadlines, injuries, schedule, equipment, how you eat and sleep. Short answers are fine; "not sure" is fine.
  4. Your first plan. A roadmap, the first block in detail, a specialist review, and then week 1 appears in Hevy in a folder called Coach.

How your plan shows up in Hevy

Hevy has no calendar, so the coach works with what Hevy does have.

  • One routine per planned session, in a folder called Coach, named like Mon - Bench heavy (wk3). The routine's note says the planned day.
  • The same routine is updated each week with that week's weights, sets and reps. Your library doesn't fill up with old weeks, and your logged history is never touched.
  • Start the workout from the routine. That is how the coach knows a session was the planned one. A workout you log from scratch on the planned day still counts; one on another day shows up as "unplanned".
  • Edit the plan by telling the coach, not in the routine: the next weekly update overwrites hand edits. "Make Thursday's accessory work lighter" works.
  • Strength only. Hevy routines hold lifting. A run or a bike ride in your plan stays in the plan and in the check-in; do it with whatever you normally use and tell the coach how it went.
  • Exercises. The coach picks from your Hevy exercise list, including your custom ones, and asks when two are close ("Pull Up" or "Pull Up (Assisted)").
  • Hevy's API can't delete routines. If the coach ever stores a routine wrongly it tells you which one to check. Deleting a routine yourself in Hevy is always fine, and the next update recreates it.

Apple Watch and other watches

Only Garmin is connected as a recovery source today. That doesn't hold you back much:

  • Hevy has its own Apple Watch app. Log your sets on your wrist and the coach reads them from Hevy like any others.
  • Sleep, HRV and resting heart rate from Apple Health, Whoop, Oura or Fitbit are not read. In their place the morning check asks about sleep, soreness, pain and stress. The sleep hours you give are saved, and after a few weeks the coach compares you with your own normal, the way a watch would.
  • Your watch still does its own job (heart rate, steps, runs). The coach just doesn't see it. Tell it about anything that matters.

Privacy

  • Your coaching folder (~/coach) and your Hevy key (~/.hevy/api-key) stay on your computer.
  • The coach's helper program talks to one place only: Hevy (api.hevyapp.com), plus git if you turn on sync between computers. No telemetry, no server of ours. It is open source: read every line in the repository.
  • The AI model reads what the coach reads: your workouts, weight, injuries and your answers. In Claude Desktop that goes to Anthropic, under your plan's terms. Check your privacy settings, in particular whether chats may be used for training. The coach only needs what you choose to tell it: a name is optional and no medical history is required.
  • The Hevy key can read and write your Hevy account. Treat it like a password; revoke it in Hevy's Developer settings if you ever think it leaked.
  • More detail: Security and privacy in the repository README.

If something doesn't work

SymptomTry
The coach says the Hevy key is missing or rejectedRun the setup command from step 1 again; it asks for the key again. Check the key in Hevy's Developer settings, and that the account is Pro.
Desktop doesn't offer the coach's tools, or "the coach server isn't available"Run the step 1 command again (it re-prepares uv and the helper), restart Claude Desktop, and start a new Cowork task. The helper is a local program, so work in Cowork on your own computer rather than a plain chat or a cloud session.
"command not found" or "not recognized" after setupClose the terminal, open a new one, run the command again.
A routine in Hevy looks wrongTell the coach which one. It re-sends it and reads it back; if Hevy still stores it differently, it tells you exactly what differs.
"Routine limit" errorThe Hevy account isn't Pro. See below.
You'd rather use the terminalThe same coach runs in Claude Code, Codex and opencode (see the README quick start). Answer no to Garmin and yes to Hevy.

Without Hevy Pro, or without Hevy

The coach still works. Answer no to the Hevy question in setup:

  • You get the plan, the weekly check-ins and the morning check, all driven by conversation. The coach writes each week's sessions out in the chat (and in your plan/ folder) for you to follow or copy into the app you use.
  • After a session, tell it how it went (loads, reps, effort). It uses that the same way it would use Hevy's log.
  • If you get Hevy Pro later, say "I use Hevy now" and it switches on.
StatusThe Hevy connection follows Hevy's published API and is tested against a simulated Hevy, not yet against many real accounts. Hevy states that its API may change. If a routine or a sync misbehaves, tell the coach and open an issue.
